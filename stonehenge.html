🪨 Stonehenge

Circle of the Ancients

Stonehenge is one of the most iconic and mysterious structures in the world. Located on the Salisbury Plain in southern England, it is composed of massive standing stones that weigh several tons each. These stones were arranged in a circular pattern, and their precise alignment with the solstices suggests that Stonehenge may have been used as a type of astronomical observatory.

Despite its significance, historians and archaeologists are still unsure how the prehistoric builders managed to move and position the stones, some of which came from over 150 miles away. The methods used to transport and erect these monolithic stones remain a topic of much debate, with theories ranging from simple wooden sledges to more complex systems of levers and ramps.

The exact purpose of Stonehenge also remains elusive. Some theories suggest that it was a religious site, while others believe it was used for healing or burial rituals. Some even propose that it was a place of astronomical observation, where ancient people tracked the movements of the sun, moon, and stars. Its true meaning is still a mystery, making it one of the world’s most fascinating monuments.
