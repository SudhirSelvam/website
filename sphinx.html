🐾 The Great Sphinx of Giza

A Monumental Mystery

Standing proudly near the Great Pyramid of Giza in Egypt, the Great Sphinx is one of the most iconic monuments of ancient Egypt. This enormous statue, which features the body of a lion and the head of a Pharaoh, is over 4,500 years old. Its exact purpose and the identity of the Pharaoh it represents remain uncertain, but it is widely believed to be a symbol of royal power and divine protection.

One of the biggest mysteries surrounding the Sphinx is its age. While it is commonly thought to have been built during the reign of Pharaoh Khafre (around 2500 BCE), some researchers believe that the Sphinx may be much older, potentially predating the pyramids themselves. Some even suggest that it was built by a lost civilization, long before the time of the Egyptians.

The Sphinx has also been the subject of numerous theories regarding hidden chambers or tunnels beneath the statue, some claiming that it may conceal valuable artifacts or secret knowledge. In the 1990s, a survey revealed that there are indeed passages under the Sphinx, but no significant discoveries have been made to date.
