🌊 The Yonaguni Monument

Submerged Ruins or Natural Formation?

Off the coast of Yonaguni, Japan, lies an underwater structure that has fascinated archaeologists and researchers for decades. The Yonaguni Monument is a series of massive, rectangular stone formations that some believe to be the remnants of an ancient, submerged city, while others argue that they are the result of natural geological processes.

The site is located about 80 feet below sea level, and it features several large platforms and terraces, along with what appear to be staircases and right angles. If the structure was man-made, it would predate any known human civilization in the region by thousands of years. Some proponents of the ancient city theory suggest that the monument may be evidence of a lost civilization, possibly linked to Atlantis.

However, skeptics argue that the monument is the result of natural tectonic activity, as the area lies near a fault line. The debate continues to this day, with no definitive answer as to whether the Yonaguni Monument is a man-made structure or a natural phenomenon.
